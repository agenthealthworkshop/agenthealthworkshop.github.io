01 / Research agenda

From answers to actions.

Healthcare agents retrieve evidence, navigate clinical records, coordinate tools, and reason over multiple steps. Biomedical agents analyze molecular data, synthesize literature, and propose experiments. Progress in both settings depends on more than a correct final answer.

AgentHealth connects machine learning, biomedical informatics, biostatistics, and public health with clinical and industry practice. We focus on the learning problems that these communities share—and on how to evaluate the decisions agents make along the way.

Topics of interest

  • Clinical workflows and patient-facing agents
  • Agents for biomedical and scientific discovery
  • Knowledge, memory, and multimodal grounding
  • Multi-agent systems and expert collaboration
  • Realistic benchmarks and distribution shift
  • Uncertainty, privacy, fairness, and governance
  • Public and population health
Q1

Grounding

How should agents ground long-horizon decisions in evolving biomedical knowledge—and recognize when that grounding is insufficient?

Q2

Evaluation

How can we evaluate agents so that benchmark results predict performance in real clinical and discovery workflows?

Q3

Expert oversight

Where should humans stay in the loop, and how can agents make expert oversight effective?

02 / Call for contributions

Bring work that benefits from discussion.

We welcome new methods, systems, benchmarks, negative results, analyses, and position papers. The following plans are subject to workshop acceptance.

Regular papers

Up to 8 pages

New methods, systems, evaluations, and substantive research arguments on agentic AI for healthcare or biomedicine.

Short papers

Up to 4 pages

Work in progress, modest results, re-analyses, dataset notes, and case studies. We particularly welcome students and clinical or biomedical researchers new to ML venues.

Page limits exclude references and appendices. Both tracks are non-archival. Accepted papers will be public on OpenReview.

Review, authorship, and presentation policies

Review

Double-blind review on OpenReview, with at least two human reviews per paper. We assess relevance, soundness, evaluation validity, limitations, and clarity.

Original and ongoing work

Papers already published at major ML conferences are out of scope. Concurrent submission is permitted where the other venue allows it.

AI and data

Papers must be primarily human-authored. AI assistance must be disclosed under the workshop policy. AI-generated short papers are not permitted. Submissions must not contain protected health information.

Participation and access

At least one author is expected to present in person. Exceptional travel constraints may be accommodated with a recorded presentation or a poster displayed on the author’s behalf.

03 / Invited perspectives

Across the clinic and the lab.

Tentative invited participants. Talk titles and participation will be finalized after workshop acceptance and confirmation.

Closing discussion panel · 40 minutes

What will it take to trust agents in the clinic and the lab?

The proposed panel will connect disagreements from the case clinic with concrete research problems and needed evaluation resources. We plan three or four external panelists, including a practicing clinician, with an organizer serving as moderator. The panel roster will be announced once confirmed.

A working session / 60 minutes

The Agent
Case Clinic.

Inspect the trajectory.
Find the first unsupported step.
Decide where an expert should intervene.

Mixed groups of domain experts and ML researchers will examine two recorded agent trajectories: one clinical case and one biomedical discovery case. Each case includes tool calls, retrieved evidence, and the agent’s final output.

  1. 10 minIntroduction and case framing
  2. 20 minClinical workflow: evidence, tools, and deferral
  3. 20 minDiscovery workflow: hypotheses and validation
  4. 10 minCompare judgments and identify oversight checkpoints

Only synthetic or publicly released data will be used. Cases, answer keys, and a participant-informed failure taxonomy will be published within two weeks of the workshop.
